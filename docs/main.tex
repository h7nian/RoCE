\begin{abstract}
High-dimensional observational data, such as electronic health records (EHRs), present distinctive challenges for causal inference and comparative effectiveness research. When patient-level data are distributed across multiple clinical sites and covariate distributions differ across sites, naive pooling or isolated site-specific analyses can yield unstable or biased estimates of target-population treatment effects. We develop a federated adaptive causal estimation framework for high-dimensional covariates (FACE-HD), extending Federated Adaptive Causal Estimation (FACE) to settings where confounding adjustment must be learned from many candidate variables. The contribution is threefold. First, FACE-HD uses a merged propensity score--density ratio calibration model, replacing separate treatment and transport models by a single high-dimensional tilting parameter for each source-treatment pair. Second, it combines two-step penalized nuisance estimation, calibration, sample splitting, and cross-fitting to construct doubly robust estimators of the target average treatment effect (TATE). Third, it provides a communication-efficient federated algorithm and a variance-aware adaptive aggregation rule that borrows information from transportable sources while downweighting non-transportable ones. Under sparsity, overlap, and regularity conditions, FACE-HD yields root-$N$ consistent and asymptotically normal estimators when either the outcome regression model or the merged propensity score--density ratio model is correctly specified, and it attains semiparametric efficiency when both are correct. Simulation studies evaluate the efficiency gains and robustness of FACE-HD relative to distributed and target-only alternatives.
\end{abstract}

\noindent{\it Keywords:} causal inference, federated learning, high-dimensional inference, transfer learning, doubly robust estimation

\section{Introduction}

The increasing availability of real-world data, including electronic health records (EHRs), administrative claims, and patient registries, offers an unprecedented opportunity to estimate causal effects in large and diverse populations \citep{hernan2016using,sherman2016real}. In practice, however, these data rarely arrive as one clean pooled dataset. Patient-level records are often held locally by clinical sites because of privacy, governance, and infrastructure constraints, while the number of candidate confounders can be large enough that standard low-dimensional causal methods become unstable \citep{belloni2014inference,chernozhukov2018double,tan2020model}. Multi-site observational studies add a further complication: covariate distributions may shift across institutions, so a source site that is useful for precision can also be harmful if its population is not transportable to the target site \citep{cole2010generalizing,pearl2014external,dahabreh2019generalizing}.

In this paper, we extend FACE to high-dimensional settings. The proposed FACE-HD framework begins from the target-site estimand, the TATE, and treats source sites as auxiliary information that must earn its way into the final estimator. For each target-source pair, FACE-HD uses a merged propensity score--density ratio calibration model, so transport and treatment weighting are handled by one exponential-tilting nuisance parameter rather than by two separately estimated high-dimensional models. It then fits the outcome regression and the merged propensity score--density ratio model through a two-step penalized calibration procedure with sample splitting and cross-fitting. This construction makes the pairwise source-assisted estimator doubly robust: first-order nuisance bias is removed when either the outcome regression or the weighting model is correctly specified, in the spirit of classical doubly robust and semiparametric estimating equations \citep{robins1994estimation,bang2005doubly,tsiatis2006semiparametric}.

The final step is adaptive aggregation. Source-assisted estimators share the same target sample, so their errors are correlated through target-side outcome-regression components. FACE-HD estimates these covariance terms explicitly and chooses aggregation weights by minimizing an estimated variance with an adaptive penalty based on disagreement between the target-only and source-assisted estimators. The result is a federated procedure that sends summary statistics and fitted nuisance parameters rather than patient-level records, borrows strength from transportable sources, and shrinks toward the target-only estimator when source information appears unreliable.

The remainder of the paper follows this story. \cref{sec:related} discusses related work in high-dimensional causal inference, transportability, and federated multi-site estimation. \cref{sec:method} introduces the multi-site notation, target estimand, calibration weights, pairwise doubly robust estimators, cross-calibrated nuisance losses, adaptive aggregation objective, federated communication protocol, and tuning rules. \cref{sec:theory} gives the regularity conditions and asymptotic guarantees behind the method, \cref{sec:simulations} describes the simulation study, and \cref{sec:discussion} closes with implications and limitations. Proofs and additional technical details are provided in the Supplemental Material.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

FACE-HD builds on the high-dimensional causal inference literature, where regularized nuisance estimation must be combined with bias correction to support valid inference. Double-selection, debiased, and double machine learning methods show how outcome and propensity-score nuisance fits can be used without letting first-stage regularization bias dominate the target effect estimator \citep{belloni2014inference,chernozhukov2018double}. For augmented inverse probability weighting and doubly robust estimation, the same theme appears through orthogonal estimating equations: if the first-order sensitivity to the nuisance functions is removed, the final estimator can remain valid when one nuisance model is correctly specified and the product of nuisance errors is small \citep{robins1994estimation,bang2005doubly,tsiatis2006semiparametric,tan2020model,smucler2019unifying}. FACE-HD follows this orthogonalization logic, but the nuisance functions are fitted under federated information constraints and are calibrated to a target-site distribution rather than to a single pooled population.

A second line of work studies generalizability, transportability, and multi-site causal inference. When source and target populations differ, source data can improve precision only if the relevant outcome and treatment-response structure transports after adjustment for observed covariates \citep{cole2010generalizing,pearl2014external,dahabreh2019generalizing}. This creates the negative-transfer problem: borrowing from a non-transportable source may reduce variance while introducing bias. The original FACE framework addresses this problem by adaptively aggregating target-only and source-assisted estimators for the TATE \citep{han2025federated}. FACE-HD keeps the FACE idea of adaptive borrowing, but extends it to high-dimensional confounding adjustment and explicitly accounts for the covariance induced by shared target data across source-assisted estimators.

Finally, the communication pattern is related to federated learning and privacy-preserving distributed analysis, where sites exchange model updates or low-dimensional summaries instead of individual-level records \citep{mcmahan2017communication,kairouz2021advances}. Recent work has begun to study federated or distributed ATE estimation directly, including differentially private multi-site aggregation of site-level treatment-effect estimates \citep{koga2024differentially}, one-shot and multi-shot federated plug-in estimators beyond meta-analysis \citep{khellaf2024federated}, and federated propensity-score aggregation for IPW and AIPW estimation \citep{khellaf2025federated}. Closely related high-dimensional distributed causal methods calibrate propensity score and outcome models under covariate shift \citep{wang2024communication,Tong2025disc2o}, while multiply robust federated estimators use transfer-learning ideas to combine information across sites without pooling records \citep{Han2023multiply}. FACE-HD differs by merging the treatment and density-ratio components into a single exponential-tilting calibration model for each source-treatment pair, then using a variance- and discrepancy-aware aggregation rule to decide how much each source should contribute to the target estimand.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

We address a causal inference problem involving a single target site ($t$) and multiple source sites ($\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_K\}$). Our primary objective is to estimate the target average treatment effect (TATE) by leveraging data from both the target site and auxiliary information from the source sites, following the target-population perspective used in generalizability and FACE-style multi-site estimation \citep{cole2010generalizing,dahabreh2019generalizing,han2025federated}.
Throughout, $\E_r[\cdot]$ denotes expectation conditional on site membership $R=r$.
We define the target-site potential outcome means
$\mu^1 \coloneqq \E_t[Y(1)]$ and $\mu^0 \coloneqq \E_t[Y(0)]$, and the TATE
\[
\tau \coloneqq \mu^1-\mu^0.
\]
Throughout the remainder of the Method section, unless explicitly stated otherwise, we develop the construction for the treated potential-outcome mean $\mu^1$. Thus the nuisance losses, influence-function components, aggregation summaries, and algorithmic steps below are written for $\mu^1$; the corresponding objects for $\mu^0$ are obtained by symmetry, replacing treatment level $1$ by $0$ and using the corresponding nuisance parameters. The TATE estimator is then obtained by differencing:
\[
\widehat{\tau} \coloneqq \widehat{\mu}^1-\widehat{\mu}^0.
\]

Each observation contains site membership $R \in \{t,s_1,\ldots,s_K\}$, raw covariates $\boldsymbol{X}$, treatment $A\in\{0,1\}$, and outcome $Y$. We write $\mathcal{D}_t$ and $\mathcal{D}_{s_j}$ for the target and source samples, with sizes $N_t$ and $N_{s_j}$, and $N_{\mathrm{all}}\coloneqq N_t+\sum_{j=1}^K N_{s_j}$. The empirical average over site $r$ is denoted by $\tildeE_r(\cdot)$. To allow flexible high-dimensional modeling, the nuisance models use a fixed working feature map $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$; one may also read $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$ simply as the transformed covariate vector used by the analysis.

For each source site $s_j \in \mathcal{S}$ and treatment level $a \in \{0,1\}$, we construct \emph{calibration weights} that reweight the $(R=s_j, A=a)$ group to match the target covariate distribution. We use an \emph{exponential tilting} (log-linear) \emph{working model} for the tilting function, in line with calibrated weighting and density-ratio ideas for high-dimensional treatment-effect estimation \citep{tan2020model,Tong2025disc2o,wang2024communication}:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:site_membership}
    r_{s_j,a}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,a}) \;\coloneqq\; \exp\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,a}\}.
\end{equation}
This form is convenient because it yields convex calibration losses and strictly positive weights. We do \emph{not} interpret \cref{eq:site_membership} as a literal probabilistic model for $P(R,A\mid \boldsymbol{X})$; instead, the key requirement for our theory is that the implied weights satisfy the covariate-balance identity defined below.
Crucially, for the pairwise DR estimator $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$, only the single parameter vector $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}$ (for the treated group at source $s_j$) is required.

Given \cref{eq:site_membership}, we define the (unnormalized) calibration weight for source-site treated observations as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:weight_def}
    w(\boldsymbol{X}; \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}) \;\coloneqq\; I(A=1)\,\exp\{-\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}\} 
    \;=\; \frac{I(A=1)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}}}.
\end{equation}
Because $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$ includes an intercept, any multiplicative normalizing constant (equivalently, an additive constant in the log-tilt) is absorbed into the intercept component of $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}$. Consequently, when $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}^*$ is the population solution of the calibration/balancing equation, the operational weight satisfies the \emph{balancing property}: for every integrable function $h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balancing_property}
    \E_{s_j}\!\bigl[w(\boldsymbol{X}; \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}^*)\,h(\boldsymbol{X})\bigr] = \E_t\!\bigl[h(\boldsymbol{X})\bigr],
\end{equation}
which is the key identity underlying the pairwise doubly robust estimator introduced below. The identity for \emph{all} integrable $h$ corresponds to the ideal population density-ratio relationship. In the high-dimensional analysis, balance is enforced over a rich but finite-dimensional function class, typically the linear span of the chosen features $h(\boldsymbol{X})\in\mathrm{span}\{\phi_1(\boldsymbol{X}),\ldots,\phi_p(\boldsymbol{X})\}$ (including an intercept). The corresponding ``weight-model correctness'' condition in the Supplemental Material should be interpreted relative to this function class.

\begin{remark}[Calibration weights vs.\ full probabilistic model]
The exponential-tilting form \cref{eq:site_membership} can be motivated by a multinomial logistic model for $(R,A\mid \boldsymbol{X})$ (with $R=t$ as a reference category), in which case the implied weights approximate inverse odds of site/treatment membership.
In this paper, however, we treat \cref{eq:site_membership} as a \emph{calibration working model}: $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,a}$ is defined by the (regularized) calibration loss, and the substantive requirement is the resulting covariate-balance moment condition \cref{eq:balancing_property}.
\end{remark}

We use generalized linear models for the potential outcomes. Let $\psi(\cdot; \cdot)$ be a GLM response function with a canonical link, mapping features to the conditional mean of the outcome.
\begin{equation*}
    m^a(\boldsymbol{X}; \boldsymbol{\alpha}^a) = \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\alpha}^a), \quad a \in \{0,1\}
\end{equation*}

The doubly robust estimators combine outcome modeling and inverse probability weighting, following the classical AIPW/semiparametric estimating-equation construction \citep{robins1994estimation,bang2005doubly,tsiatis2006semiparametric}. The estimator for $\mu^1$ assisted by source site $s_j$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source_assisted_mu1}
\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j} = \tildeE_{t}\bigl(\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1)\bigr) + \tildeE_{s_j}\left(\frac{I(A = 1)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}}} \bigl(Y - \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1)\bigr)\right)
\end{equation}
The estimator for the control group, $\widehat{\mu}^0_{t,s_j}$, is defined symmetrically by using the nuisance parameters $(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^0,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,0})$ and replacing $I(A=1)$ by $I(A=0)$ in the weighted residual term:
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\mu}^0_{t,s_j} = \tildeE_{t}\bigl(\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^0)\bigr) + \tildeE_{s_j}\left(\frac{I(A = 0)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,0}}} \bigl(Y - \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^0)\bigr)\right).
\end{equation*}
Finally, the (pairwise) TATE estimator is $\widehat{\tau}_{t,s_j}\coloneqq \widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}-\widehat{\mu}^0_{t,s_j}$, and the target-only TATE estimator is $\widehat{\tau}_{ot}\coloneqq \widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}-\widehat{\mu}^0_{ot}$.

The target-only estimator $\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}$ is a standard AIPW estimator on the target data alone and requires the \emph{target-site propensity score},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi_ot}
\pi_{ot}(\boldsymbol{X}) \coloneqq P(A=1 \mid R=t,\, \boldsymbol{X}),
\end{equation}
which models treatment assignment \emph{within} the target population.
This is conceptually and operationally distinct from the cross-site calibration/tilting parameters $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,a}$ defined above:
$\pi_{ot}$ is estimated locally at the target site (e.g., via $\ell_1$-penalized logistic regression on $(A,\boldsymbol{X})$ pairs from $\mathcal{D}_t$), and does not enter the source-assisted estimator $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$ or its loss functions.

\subsection{Cross-Calibrated Nuisance Estimation}\label{sec:nuisance}

In this framework, the outcome model parameters $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^a$ and the site/treatment model parameters $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,a}$ are interdependent. This coupling arises because the optimal choice for $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ depends on weights derived from $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$, and vice-versa. Solving this coupled system jointly is computationally challenging in a federated setting where data is decentralized.

To derive loss functions for a federated, iterative estimation process, we follow the principle of constructing estimators that solve efficient score equations, as identified by influence-function and orthogonal estimating-equation analysis \citep{tsiatis2006semiparametric,chernozhukov2018double,tan2020model,smucler2019unifying}.

Let $\boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1$ and $\boldsymbol{\bar{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}$ denote the population targets (probability limits / pseudo-true parameters) associated with the nuisance estimation step; under correct specification of the corresponding nuisance model, these coincide with the true parameters. Let $\bar{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$ be the source-assisted functional obtained by evaluating the pairwise estimator at these population targets. The asymptotic error of $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$ can be decomposed into terms related to the estimation errors of the parameters $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ and $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:if_decomposition}
\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j} - \bar{\mu}^1_{t,s_j} = (\boldsymbol{\widehat{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1 - \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1)^T \boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha} + (\boldsymbol{\widehat{\gamma}}_{s_j,1} - \boldsymbol{\bar{\gamma}}_{s_j,1})^T \boldsymbol{U}_{\gamma} + \text{h.o.t.}
\end{equation}
The vectors $\boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha}$ and $\boldsymbol{U}_{\gamma}$ are the score vectors:
\begin{align} \label{eq:score_vectors}
\boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha} &= \tildeE_{t} \left[ \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1) \right] - \tildeE_{s_j} \left[\frac{I(A = 1)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\bar{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}}} \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1) \right] \\
\boldsymbol{U}_{\gamma} &= \tildeE_{s_j}  \left[ -\frac{I(A = 1)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\bar{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}}} \phi(\boldsymbol{X}) (Y - \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1)) \right]
\end{align}
 
For the remainder in \cref{eq:if_decomposition} to be second-order in the nuisance errors (Neyman orthogonality / debiasing), the estimators must be constructed so that $\boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha} = \mathbf{0}$ \emph{at} $\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$ and $\boldsymbol{U}_{\gamma} = \mathbf{0}$ \emph{at} $\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}$.
The cross-calibration strategy achieves this by constructing the loss for $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ so that its first-order condition \emph{is} the score equation $\boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{0}$, and symmetrically for $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$.

Let $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{init}}$ and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init}}$ denote initial plug-in estimates of the outcome and site/treatment parameters, respectively. To ensure numerical stability and satisfy the positivity regularity conditions used in the theory, define the truncation function
\[
\mathcal{T}(x) \coloneqq \mathrm{sign}(x)\min\{|x|,M_{\tau}\},
\]
where $M_{\tau}$ may depend on sample size and is chosen sufficiently large so truncation is asymptotically inactive at the population limit. The subscript recalls the TATE parameter $\tau$ defined above, and helps distinguish this numerical threshold from the treatment level.
For exponential-family GLMs with canonical link, write the negative log-likelihood as $-Y\theta+\Psi(\theta)$, where $\theta=\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ is the linear predictor, $\psi(\theta)=\Psi'(\theta)$ is the conditional mean, and $\psi'(\theta)$ denotes the derivative with respect to $\theta$.
Let $\lambda_{\gamma}\ge0$ and $\lambda_{\alpha}\ge0$ denote the $\ell_1$ regularization parameters for the site/treatment and outcome calibrated losses, respectively.

The calibrated exponential-tilting loss for $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\gamma, \mathrm{cal}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}; \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{init}})
&= \tildeE_t \left[ \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{init}})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1} \right] \\
&\quad + \tildeE_{s_j} \left[ I(A=1) e^{-\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}} \psi'(\mathcal{T}(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{init}})) \right] \\
&\quad + \lambda_{\gamma} \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}\|_1 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Symmetrically, the calibrated weighted GLM loss for $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\alpha, \mathrm{cal}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1; \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init}})
&= \tildeE_{s_j} \left[ \frac{I(A=1)}{e^{\mathcal{T}(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init}})}} \left\{ -Y \cdot (\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1) + \Psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1) \right\} \right] \\
&\quad + \lambda_{\alpha} \|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1\|_1 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

To verify the cross-calibration, take the gradient of \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss} with respect to $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ (setting aside the $\ell_1$ subdifferential):
\[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} \ell = \tildeE_t\!\left[ \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}) \right] - \tildeE_{s_j}\!\left[ \frac{I(A=1)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}}} \,\psi'(\mathcal{T}(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}}))\,\phi(\boldsymbol{X}) \right].
\]
For a canonical-link GLM, $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \psi'(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\alpha})\,\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, so under asymptotically inactive truncation the two terms become
\[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}} \ell = \tildeE_t\!\left[\psi'(\phi^T \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}})\,\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\right] - \tildeE_{s_j}\!\left[w(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{\gamma})\,\psi'(\phi^T \boldsymbol{\bar{\alpha}})\,\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\right],
\]
which matches the score vector $\boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha}$ in \cref{eq:score_vectors} up to an asymptotically negligible truncation term.
\emph{Hence the population first-order condition of the calibrated $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$-loss enforces the same first-order orthogonality equation} $\boldsymbol{U}_{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{0}$.
Moreover, the calibrated loss \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss} is strictly convex in $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ (because $t\mapsto e^{-t}$ is convex), guaranteeing a unique minimizer under standard regularity.

The outcome model is fitted by minimizing \cref{eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}, given a plug-in site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}$.
The corresponding gradient is
\[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \ell = \tildeE_{s_j}\!\left[ \frac{I(A=1)}{e^{\mathcal{T}(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})}}\, \bigl(-Y + \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\alpha})\bigr)\,\phi(\boldsymbol{X}) \right],
\]
and under asymptotically inactive truncation this matches $-\,\boldsymbol{U}_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})$ at first order, so the first-order condition $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \ell = \boldsymbol{0}$ enforces $\boldsymbol{U}_{\gamma}=\boldsymbol{0}$.

Together, the first-order terms in the remainder \cref{eq:if_decomposition} vanish at the population level, leaving a second-order error $O_p(\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^*\|\cdot\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}^*\|)$ that is negligible under standard high-dimensional rates \citep{buhlmann2011statistics,negahban2012unified}; see the Supplemental Material for details.

In practice we first obtain robust initial estimates to bootstrap the coupled problem. A simple $\ell_1$-regularized regression on source-site treated data provides the starting outcome model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(0)}$ \citep{hastie2009elements,buhlmann2011statistics}. Let $\ell_{GLM}$ be the standard loss for the chosen GLM (e.g., squared error for linear outcomes or log-loss for binary outcomes):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha_init}
\ell\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^{1}\right) = \tildeE_{s_j}\left[I(A=1)\ell_{GLM}\left(Y, \psi\left(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1\right)\right)\right] + \lambda_{\alpha} \|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^{1}\|_1
\end{equation}

The initial site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,a}^{(0)}$ is obtained from a regularized exponential-tilting loss that targets covariate balance without depending on the outcome model:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gamma_init}
\ell(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}) = \left( \tildeE_t \left[ \phi(\boldsymbol{X}) \right] \right)^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1} + \tildeE_{s_j} \left[ I(A=1) e^{-\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}} \right] + \lambda_{\gamma} \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}\|_1
\end{equation}

Using these initial estimates, the source site performs calibrated updates in a two-level cross-fitted way, combining the general cross-fitting idea from double/debiased machine learning with the SMMAL-style two-level calibration logic used in semi-supervised ATE estimation \citep{chernozhukov2018double,hou2025efficient}. For an outer evaluation fold $k_1$, an initial nuisance plug-in is trained for each secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$ using data excluding both $k_1$ and $k_2$. The final calibrated nuisance estimator for fold $k_1$ is then obtained by minimizing the average of the fold-specific calibrated losses over all $k_2\neq k_1$: the $k_2$ loss contribution is evaluated on fold $k_2$ and uses the initial plug-in fitted on the remaining training folds. Thus calibration uses the full outer-training sample $\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ through $K_f-1$ fold-specific loss contributions, while the final evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ remains untouched. The plug-in order is intentional in the two-level cross-fitting construction and does not affect first-order asymptotics under the alignment condition in the Supplemental Material.

\subsection{Influence Functions and Adaptive Aggregation}

To construct confidence intervals and find optimal aggregation weights, we rely on the influence functions (IF) of our estimators \citep{tsiatis2006semiparametric}. The total variance of the final aggregated estimator is a function of the variances and covariances of these IFs.

Let $\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}$ be the target-only estimator and $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$ be the source-assisted estimator from site $s_j$.

For the target-only estimator, using the target-site propensity score $\widehat{\pi}_{ot}(\boldsymbol{X})$ defined in \cref{eq:pi_ot} and a target-only outcome model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^1$, we estimate
\begin{equation} \label{eq:if_target_only}
\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} = \frac{A_i Y_i}{\widehat{\pi}_{ot}(\boldsymbol{X}_i)} - \left(\frac{A_i}{\widehat{\pi}_{ot}(\boldsymbol{X}_i)} - 1\right)\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^1) - \widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}
\end{equation}
The source-assisted estimator has a target-side regression component,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:if_target_component}
\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i} = \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1) - \tildeE_t(\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1))
\end{equation}
and a source-side weighted residual component, using the calibration weight $w(\boldsymbol{X}; \boldsymbol{\gamma})$ from \cref{eq:weight_def},
\begin{equation} \label{eq:if_source_component}
\widehat{\xi}_{t,s_j,i} = \frac{A_i}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i)^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}}}(Y_i - \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1)) - \widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^1
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\delta}^1_{t,s_j} = \tildeE_{s_j} \left(\frac{A}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}}} (Y - \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1))\right)$.

These pieces lead directly to the variance and covariance components:
\begin{align*}
    \widehat{V}_{ot} &= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} - \bar{\widehat{\varphi}}_{ot})^2 \\
    \widehat{V}_{t,s_j} &= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i} - \bar{\widehat{\zeta}}_{t,s_j})^2 \\
    \widehat{V}_{s_j} &= \frac{1}{N_{s_j}}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_{s_j}}(\widehat{\xi}_{t,s_j,i} - \bar{\widehat{\xi}}_{t,s_j})^2 \\
    \widehat{C}_{ot,s_j} &= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} - \bar{\widehat{\varphi}}_{ot})(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i} - \bar{\widehat{\zeta}}_{t,s_j})
\end{align*}
One extra covariance term is easy to miss. All source-assisted estimators reuse the same target data, so the target-side components across two sources are correlated:
\begin{equation*}
    \widehat{C}_{t,s_j,s_k} = \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i} - \bar{\widehat{\zeta}}_{t,s_j})(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_k,i} - \bar{\widehat{\zeta}}_{t,s_k}) \quad \text{for } j \neq k
\end{equation*}
Ignoring these cross-site correlations would lead to a systematic underestimation of the aggregated estimator's variance and incorrect confidence intervals; see also the analogous shared-target covariance correction in FACE \citep{han2025federated}.

In finite samples, the plug-in variance/covariance components (especially the shared-target covariance terms) can yield a quadratic objective in \cref{eq:final_opt} that is only approximately convex due to estimation noise.
For numerical stability, the estimated covariance inputs may be symmetrized and minimally ridge-regularized when needed to keep the weight optimization well posed; this ridge vanishes asymptotically under the regularity conditions in the Supplemental Material.

The target site combines its own estimator, $\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}$, with the estimators received from all source sites, $\{\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}\}_{j=1}^K$. The final aggregated estimator is a weighted combination:
\begin{equation*}
    \widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}} = (1 - \sum_{j=1}^K \eta_j) \widehat{\mu}^1_{ot} + \sum_{j=1}^K \eta_j \widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}
\end{equation*}
The estimated weights $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}} = (\widehat{\eta}_1, \ldots, \widehat{\eta}_K)$ are found by minimizing the estimated variance of $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}}$, with an added penalty term to regularize the weights and perform source selection, following the adaptive borrowing strategy of FACE \citep{han2025federated}. This leads to the following quadratic optimization problem:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:final_opt}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\boldsymbol{\eta}} \quad & \frac{1}{N_t}\Bigl(1 - \sum_{j=1}^K \eta_j\Bigr)^2 \widehat{V}_{ot} + \sum_{j=1}^K \frac{\eta_j^2}{N_t} \widehat{V}_{t,s_j} + \sum_{j=1}^K \frac{\eta_j^2}{N_{s_j}} \widehat{V}_{s_j} \\
&+ \frac{2}{N_t}\sum_{j=1}^K \eta_j\Bigl(1 - \sum_{k=1}^K \eta_k\Bigr)\widehat{C}_{ot,s_j} + \frac{2}{N_t}\sum_{j<k} \eta_j \eta_k \widehat{C}_{t,s_j,s_k} \\
&+ \lambda \sum_{j = 1}^K\left|\eta_j\right|\left(\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}-\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}\right)^2
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\cref{eq:final_opt} is written in terms of an estimated variance $\widehat{\Var}(\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}})$.
For asymptotic analysis of $\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{all}}}(\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}}-\mu^1)$, one can equivalently work with the estimated asymptotic variance $N_{\mathrm{all}}\cdot \widehat{\Var}(\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}})$, which multiplies the variance and covariance terms in \cref{eq:final_opt} by $N_{\mathrm{all}}$.
This scaling can be absorbed into the tuning of $\lambda$ and is used in the Supplemental Material.
Define $\widehat{\mu}^0_{\text{agg}}$ by applying the same aggregation construction to $\{\widehat{\mu}^0_{t,s_j}\}_{j=1}^K$ (with the corresponding variance/covariance components for the control arm), and set $\widehat{\tau}_{\text{agg}}\coloneqq \widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}}-\widehat{\mu}^0_{\text{agg}}$. The Supplemental Material gives the asymptotic linearity/normality argument; inference for $\tau$ follows by standard linear combination (difference) of the two arm-specific influence function representations.

\subsection{Federated Algorithm}\label{sec:algorithms}

The operational version of FACE-HD is a summary-exchange algorithm. Each site keeps its individual-level records locally, while the target and source sites pass only fitted nuisance parameters, empirical moments, sample sizes, and variance summaries, consistent with the federated-learning principle of replacing record pooling by structured communication \citep{mcmahan2017communication,kairouz2021advances,han2025federated}. The default implementation in the main manuscript is a two-round algorithm with two-level cross-fitting: the two communication rounds provide source-specific initialization and calibration, while the two-level split separates the outer evaluation fold from the secondary calibration folds. The same steps are run separately for the treated and control arms; below we describe the treated arm $\mu^1$, with the control arm obtained by replacing $I(A=1)$ and the corresponding nuisance parameters by their $a=0$ analogues. A one-round version is useful when an additional communication round is operationally costly and the target-site initial outcome model is expected to be sufficiently aligned with the source-specific calibrated nuisance targets; otherwise, especially under substantial source-target heterogeneity, the two-round version is preferred because it avoids this stronger target-initialization requirement. The fold-level two-round bookkeeping, the one-round alternative, and the linear-outcome specialization are given in \cref{app:two_level_cf,app:one_round_cf,app:linear_one_round_cf}.

A compact version of the theory-aligned two-round algorithm with two-level cross-fitting is given in \Cref{alg:facehd-compact}. The subsequent paragraphs first spell out the summaries exchanged in a single training/evaluation split and then explain the fold-level implementation used for inference.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Two-Round Algorithm with Two-Level Cross-Fitting}
\label{alg:facehd-compact}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Target data $\mathcal{D}_t$, source data $\{\mathcal{D}_{s_j}\}_{j=1}^K$, feature map $\phi$, nuisance penalty grids, aggregation penalty grid, and $K_f$ two-level cross-fitting folds.
\ENSURE Aggregated TATE estimate $\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg}}$ and its variance estimate.
\STATE All sites keep individual-level records local and agree on $\phi$, fold indices, and tuning grids.
\FOR{each outer evaluation fold $k_1=1,\ldots,K_f$}
    \STATE Keep $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ held out. Learn nuisance models, tune $\lambda$, and estimate aggregation weights only inside $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$.
    \STATE Target fits the target-only nuisance models using the target propensity model in \cref{eq:pi_ot}, computes the target-only IF in \cref{eq:if_target_only}, and keeps target records local for later covariance calculations.
    \FOR{each treatment arm $a\in\{0,1\}$}
        \FOR{each source site $s_j$ in parallel}
            \STATE \textbf{Round 1.} For every secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$, source $s_j$ fits an initial outcome model on $\mathcal{D}_{s_j}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2})$ using the arm-specific version of \cref{eq:alpha_init}, and sends only the fitted parameter vector to the target.
            \STATE Target evaluates the fold-specific derivative and covariate summaries entering \cref{eq:gamma_init,eq:gamma_calibrated_loss} and returns those summaries to source $s_j$.
            \STATE \textbf{Round 2.} Source $s_j$ computes the initial site/treatment model using \cref{eq:gamma_init}, solves the fold-summed calibrated losses in \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss,eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}, and returns $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{a,(-k_1)}$, the source correction and variance based on \cref{eq:if_source_component}, and $N_{s_j}$.
        \ENDFOR
        \STATE Target forms fold-specific source-assisted estimates using the arm-specific version of \cref{eq:source_assisted_mu1}, estimates the variance/covariance components in \cref{eq:if_target_only,eq:if_target_component,eq:if_source_component}, selects aggregation weights by \cref{eq:final_opt} inside $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$, and applies those weights to $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ to compute $\widehat{\mu}_{\mathrm{agg},k_1}^a$.
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Compute $\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg},k_1}=\widehat{\mu}_{\mathrm{agg},k_1}^1-\widehat{\mu}_{\mathrm{agg},k_1}^0$ on the held-out fold.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Average fold-specific estimates and report $\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg}}=K_f^{-1}\sum_{k_1=1}^{K_f}\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg},k_1}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Local inputs.} At the beginning, the target site holds $\mathcal{D}_t$ and each source site $s_j$ holds $\mathcal{D}_{s_j}$. All sites agree on the working feature map $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, the GLM response function $\psi$, the fold partition used for two-level cross-fitting, and candidate grids for the nuisance penalties $(\lambda_\gamma,\lambda_\alpha)$ and aggregation penalty $\lambda$. The target also fits the target-only nuisance models needed for $\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}$ and its influence-function component $\widehat{\varphi}_{ot}$.

\textbf{Round 1: source initialization.} Each source site first fits an initial treated-outcome model on its own treated observations:
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(0)}
\in
\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
\left\{
\tildeE_{s_j}\!\left[I(A=1)\ell_{GLM}\{Y,\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X});\boldsymbol{\alpha})\}\right]
+\lambda_\alpha\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|_1
\right\}.
\]
The source sends only this fitted parameter vector, and not its records, to the target. For each received $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(0)}$, the target evaluates the target-side calibration summary
\[
\tildeE_t\!\left[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\psi\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X});\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(0)}\}\right],
\]
and returns this vector to the corresponding source. If the source also needs an initial site/treatment fit, the target additionally sends the target covariate mean $\tildeE_t\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}$. These quantities are low-dimensional or $p$-dimensional summaries determined by the chosen feature map, rather than individual-level observations.

\textbf{Round 2: source calibration.} After receiving the target summaries, each source solves the two calibrated nuisance problems locally. First, it estimates the merged site/treatment tilting parameter $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}$ using the calibrated exponential-tilting loss in \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss}. This step uses the target-side derivative summary from Round 1 and the source site's own treated observations. Second, using the resulting tilting model as the plug-in weight model, the source estimates the calibrated outcome parameter $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$ from \cref{eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}. This ordering is the finite-sample implementation of the cross-calibration logic: the $\gamma$ update targets covariate/score balance against the target, and the $\alpha$ update targets a weighted residual equation at the source.

The source then forms the source-side correction and variance summaries
\[
\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^1
=
\tildeE_{s_j}\!\left[
w(\boldsymbol{X};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1})
\{Y-\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X});\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1})\}
\right],
\]
and
\[
\widehat{V}_{s_j}
=
\frac{1}{N_{s_j}}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{s_j}}
\left\{
w(\boldsymbol{X}_i;\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1})
\bigl(Y_i-\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i);\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1})\bigr)
-\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^1
\right\}^2.
\]
It sends back $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$, $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^1$, $\widehat{V}_{s_j}$, and $N_{s_j}$. The site/treatment parameter $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}$ can remain local because the target does not need to reconstruct source-level residuals once the correction and variance summaries have been computed.

\textbf{Target-side assembly.} With the returned source summaries, the target builds each source-assisted estimator by combining its target-side regression average with the source correction:
\[
\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}
=
\tildeE_t\!\left[
\psi\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X});\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}\}
\right]
+\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^1.
\]
Because all source-assisted estimators use the same target data, the target also computes the target-side variance terms $\widehat{V}_{t,s_j}$, the target-only/source covariance terms $\widehat{C}_{ot,s_j}$, and the cross-source target covariance terms $\widehat{C}_{t,s_j,s_k}$ locally from $\mathcal{D}_t$ and the received outcome parameters. These pieces, together with $\widehat{V}_{ot}$ and the source-side variances, are exactly the inputs to the aggregation objective in \cref{eq:final_opt}. The target then selects $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}$, forms $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\mathrm{agg}}$, repeats the same construction for $\mu^0$, and reports
\[
\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg}}
=
\widehat{\mu}^1_{\mathrm{agg}}-\widehat{\mu}^0_{\mathrm{agg}}.
\]

\textbf{Two-level cross-fitted implementation.} The description above gives the information-flow skeleton for one training/evaluation split. The estimator analyzed in the theory uses a fold-specific, two-level cross-fitted implementation following the SMMAL-style calibration construction of \citet{hou2025efficient}. For an outer evaluation fold $k_1$, all nuisance models and aggregation weights are learned only on the outer-training data $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$. Inside $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$, each secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$ supplies a calibration fold, while the initial nuisance plug-ins for that calibration fold are trained on $\mathcal{D}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{k_2})$. The calibrated nuisance estimator for evaluating $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ is obtained by minimizing the average of these secondary-fold calibrated losses. Following the FACE validation strategy for adaptive aggregation \citep{han2025federated}, the aggregation penalty $\lambda$ is also selected inside $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$, and the final weights $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$ are applied only to observations in the held-out outer fold. \Cref{app:two_level_cf} gives the full fold-level bookkeeping, \cref{app:one_round_cf} records the communication-saving one-round version under the stronger initialization-alignment condition described above, and \cref{app:linear_one_round_cf} gives the corresponding simplification for linear outcome models.

\subsection{Cross-Validation and Tuning Parameters}\label{sec:tuning}
The framework involves two types of regularization parameters ($\lambda$) that must be tuned.

For each nuisance model (parameters $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ and $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$), the regularization parameters ($\lambda_\gamma, \lambda_\alpha$) are selected via $K_f$-fold cross-validation, where $K_f$ is a fixed constant. The criterion for selection is the minimization of the corresponding calibrated loss function (\cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss} or \cref{eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}) on the validation folds. This is performed locally at each site during the federated step where the model is estimated.

The regularization parameter $\lambda$ in the aggregation objective~\cref{eq:final_opt} controls how aggressively FACE-HD borrows from source sites. Small $\lambda$ allows more borrowing and can reduce variance when source-assisted estimators are transportable, but it also increases exposure to negative transfer. Large $\lambda$ shrinks source weights toward zero, moving the estimator toward the target-only anchor and reducing transfer-bias risk at the cost of weaker precision gains.

A fixed grid $\Lambda$ (e.g.\ $[10^{-3}, 1]$) is not robust across problem scales because the useful range of $\lambda$ depends on the magnitudes of the variance components and the site discrepancies. To construct a data-adaptive grid, we identify the \emph{natural scale} $\lambda_{\mathrm{nat}}$ at which the penalty and variance terms are comparable.

For source site $s_j$, the unpenalized variance contribution is
\[
\sigma_j^2 \;\coloneqq\; \frac{\widehat{V}_{t,s_j}}{N_t} + \frac{\widehat{V}_{s_j}}{N_{s_j}},
\]
and the penalty scale is $d_j^2 \coloneqq (\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot} - \widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j})^2$.
Setting $\lambda \, d_j^2 = \sigma_j^2$ gives the transition point
$\lambda_j = \sigma_j^2 / d_j^2$.
When $d_j^2$ is very small (i.e.\ the source and target estimates nearly agree, suggesting low bias), we floor $d_j^2$ at $\sigma_j^2$ to avoid $\lambda_j \to \infty$:
\[
\lambda_j \;=\; \frac{\sigma_j^2}{\max(d_j^2,\; \sigma_j^2)}.
\]
The natural scale is defined as
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_{\mathrm{nat}} \;=\; \operatorname{median}_{j=1,\ldots,K}\; \lambda_j,
\end{equation*}
and the search grid spans two orders of magnitude on each side:
\[
\Lambda \;=\; \bigl\{\lambda : \lambda \in [10^{-2}\,\lambda_{\mathrm{nat}},\; 10^{2}\,\lambda_{\mathrm{nat}}]\bigr\},
\]
with $|\Lambda|$ evenly spaced on the log scale (e.g.\ 100 points).
The grid is recomputed inside each outer training set and spans the transition from weakly regularized weights to strong shrinkage toward the target-only estimator.

We select $\lambda$ by the sample-splitting validation idea used in FACE \citep{han2025federated}. For an outer evaluation fold $k_1$, let $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ be the data available for nuisance fitting and weight tuning. Split $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$ into $M$ inner folds. For inner fold $m$, let $\mathcal{T}_{k_1,-m}$ be the inner-training data and $\mathcal{I}_m$ the inner-validation data, keeping site proportions approximately fixed.

For each candidate $\lambda\in\Lambda$, all nuisance parameters and variance/discrepancy summaries are computed on the inner-training data $\mathcal{T}_{k_1,-m}$, and the target solves the penalized aggregation problem:
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1,-m)}(\lambda)
=
\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
\left\{
\widehat{\Var}_{\mathrm{tr},m}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^K|\eta_j|
\left(\widehat{\mu}^{1,\mathrm{tr}}_{ot,m}-\widehat{\mu}^{1,\mathrm{tr}}_{t,s_j,m}\right)^2
\right\}.
\]
In parallel, each site computes validation summaries on $\mathcal{I}_m$ using nuisance parameters trained on $\mathcal{T}_{k_1,-m}$; no individual-level validation records are shared. The candidate $\lambda$ is evaluated by plugging the training-set weights into the validation-set penalized criterion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda_validation_loss}
Q_{\mathrm{val},m}(\lambda)
=
N_{\mathrm{val},m}\,
\widehat{\Var}_{\mathrm{val},m}\!\left\{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1,-m)}(\lambda)\right\}
+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^K
\left|\widehat{\eta}^{(-k_1,-m)}_j(\lambda)\right|
\left(\widehat{\mu}^{1,\mathrm{val}}_{ot,m}-\widehat{\mu}^{1,\mathrm{val}}_{t,s_j,m}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $N_{\mathrm{val},m}$ is the total validation sample size across sites in $\mathcal{I}_m$ and $\widehat{\Var}_{\mathrm{val},m}\{\eta\}$ is the FACE-HD variance expression in \cref{eq:final_opt} evaluated with validation-fold variance/covariance summaries and the fixed weights $\eta$.
The selected aggregation tuning parameter is
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}
=
\arg\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M Q_{\mathrm{val},m}(\lambda).
\end{equation*}
As in FACE, the split can be repeated over several inner folds (e.g., five-fold validation), and the selected value minimizes the average validation criterion. As a conservative finite-sample variant, one may choose the largest $\lambda$ whose average validation criterion is within one estimated standard error, or within a small relative tolerance, of the minimum. After $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$ is selected, the aggregation weights $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$ are recomputed on all of $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$ and then applied only to the outer evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

This section states the main theoretical guarantees in the same order as the method is built: first the conditions under which each target-source estimator is valid, then the aggregation result that protects against negative transfer, and finally the implication for the TATE. Detailed proofs, including empirical-process and selection arguments, are provided in the Supplemental Material. Throughout this section $K$ and $K_f$ are fixed, $N_{\mathrm{all}}\to\infty$, the site fractions $N_t/N_{\mathrm{all}}$ and $N_{s_j}/N_{\mathrm{all}}$ stay bounded away from zero, and the covariate dimension $p$ may grow with sample size.

\subsection{High-Level Conditions}
The first group of conditions is causal and sampling-based. We assume consistency, standard within-site treatment ignorability given $\boldsymbol{X}$ for the target estimand, and that any source site treated as informative satisfies the corresponding transportability condition after adjustment for $\boldsymbol{X}$. The target and source samples are independent, observations are i.i.d.\ within each site, and the working covariates $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$ have sub-Gaussian coordinates with an intercept included. We also assume overlap: the relevant treatment and site/treatment probabilities are bounded away from zero on the target support, and the resulting exponential-tilting weights have uniformly bounded moments of order larger than two.

The second group of conditions concerns high-dimensional nuisance estimation. Let $s_{\alpha,j}$ and $s_{\gamma,j}$ denote the sparsities of the calibrated outcome and site/treatment tilting targets for source $s_j$, and let $s_{\mathrm{tot},j}=s_{\alpha,j}+s_{\gamma,j}$. The population calibrated losses satisfy restricted strong convexity on the sparse cones induced by these targets, and the fold-specific nuisance estimators are trained on data independent of their evaluation folds. The two-level cross-fitting construction aligns the population limits of the initial plug-in estimators with the final calibrated targets. Under these conditions, the calibrated $\ell_1$ estimators obey the usual high-dimensional rates,
\[
\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^*\|_2+\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}^*\|_2
=
O_p\!\left(\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{tot},j}\log(p)/N_{t,s_j}}\right),
\]
with analogous $\ell_1$ rates of order $s_{\mathrm{tot},j}\sqrt{\log(p)/N_{t,s_j}}$, where $N_{t,s_j}=N_t+N_{s_j}$. The sparsity scaling required for the first-order theory is $s_{\mathrm{tot},j}^2\log^2(p)/N_{t,s_j}\to0$ for each informative source.

\subsection{Pairwise Source-Assisted Estimation}
For a fixed source site $s_j$ and treatment arm $a\in\{0,1\}$, the calibrated source-assisted estimator has the same first-order structure as an orthogonal doubly robust estimator. The calibration losses are designed so that the derivative of the estimating equation with respect to one nuisance component vanishes when the other nuisance component is evaluated at its calibrated target. As a result, the first-order nuisance error cancels and the remainder is second order, of size
\[
O_p\!\left(\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^*\|_2
\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}^*\|_2\right)^2
=o_p(N_{t,s_j}^{-1/2})
\]
under the sparsity scaling above.

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise doubly robust asymptotic normality]
Under the high-level conditions above, suppose that for source $s_j$ at least one of the two nuisance models is correctly specified for arm $a$: either the calibrated outcome regression equals the target-site conditional mean, or the exponential-tilting weight satisfies the target-source balancing identity for the relevant function class. If $s_{\mathrm{tot},j}^2\log^2(p)/N_{t,s_j}\to0$ and the asymptotic variance $V^a_{t,s_j}$ is positive, then
\[
\sqrt{N_{t,s_j}}\bigl(\widehat{\mu}^{a}_{t,s_j}-\mu^a\bigr)
\xrightarrow{d}
\mathcal{N}(0,V^a_{t,s_j}).
\]
The plug-in variance estimator based on the cross-fitted influence-function components is consistent for $V^a_{t,s_j}$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem gives the pairwise protection that FACE-HD needs before aggregation: a source can be useful even if one nuisance model is misspecified, provided the other nuisance model identifies the target mean and the high-dimensional nuisance rates are fast enough. Applying the same statement to $a=1$ and $a=0$ yields the pairwise TATE estimator $\widehat{\tau}_{t,s_j}=\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}-\widehat{\mu}^0_{t,s_j}$, whose variance is obtained from the difference of the two arm-specific influence functions.

\subsection{Adaptive Aggregation and Source Selection}
The aggregation step decides how much each source-assisted estimator should contribute after accounting for both variance and possible bias. For arm $a$, define the informative source set as $\mathcal{S}^{a,*}=\{j:\operatorname{plim}\widehat{\mu}^a_{t,s_j}=\mu^a\}$. Let $\eta^{a,*}$ be the oracle minimizer of the population asymptotic variance over weights supported only on $\mathcal{S}^{a,*}$, with the target-only estimator included through the weight $1-\sum_j\eta_j$.

The aggregation theory assumes that the population quadratic variance is locally strictly convex on the oracle subspace, that non-informative sources are separated from the target by a beta-min condition, and that the aggregation penalty satisfies $\lambda\to\infty$ while $\lambda/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{all}}}\to0$. Under fixed separation, the penalty dominates the discrepancy term for biased sources and sets their weights to zero with probability tending to one; for informative sources, the penalty is asymptotically negligible at the $N_{\mathrm{all}}^{-1/2}$ scale.

\begin{theorem}[FACE-HD aggregation]
Suppose the pairwise conditions above hold for every informative source, the aggregation regularity conditions hold, and $\max_{j\in\mathcal{S}^{a,*}}s_{\mathrm{tot},j}^2\log^2(p)/N_{t,s_j}\to0$. Then the adaptive aggregation weights satisfy $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^a-\boldsymbol{\eta}^{a,*}\|_2=O_p(N_{\mathrm{all}}^{-1/2})$ on the oracle subspace and $\widehat{\eta}^a_j=0$ for all $j\notin\mathcal{S}^{a,*}$ with probability tending to one. Consequently,
\[
\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{all}}}\bigl(\widehat{\mu}^a_{\mathrm{agg}}-\mu^a\bigr)
\xrightarrow{d}
\mathcal{N}\{0,L_a^*(\eta^{a,*})\},
\]
where $L_a^*(\eta^{a,*})$ is the oracle minimum asymptotic variance. The estimated aggregation variance $\widehat{L}_a(\widehat{\eta}^a)$ is consistent for $L_a^*(\eta^{a,*})$.
\end{theorem}

This result is the high-dimensional analogue of the oracle property used in FACE. The data-driven procedure behaves, to first order, as if the set of transportable sources were known in advance and the optimal variance-minimizing weights were computed on that oracle set. If some biased sources are local alternatives whose bias is $o(N_{\mathrm{all}}^{-1/2})$, exact selection may fail, but their contribution is still negligible for first-order inference.

\subsection{Inference for the TATE and Efficiency}
The final estimand is the TATE $\tau=\mu^1-\mu^0$. Applying the aggregation theorem to the treated and control arms and taking the difference gives
\[
\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{all}}}\bigl(\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg}}-\tau\bigr)
\xrightarrow{d}
\mathcal{N}(0,V_\tau^*),
\]
where $V_\tau^*$ is the variance of the difference between the treated- and control-arm oracle influence functions. The corresponding Wald interval is
\[
\widehat{\tau}_{\mathrm{agg}}
\pm
z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat{V}_\tau/N_{\mathrm{all}}},
\]
with $\widehat{V}_\tau$ computed from the same cross-fitted influence-function representation used for the point estimator.

Because the target-only estimator corresponds to the feasible aggregation choice $\eta=0$, the oracle aggregation variance is no larger than the target-only asymptotic variance. It is strictly smaller whenever at least one informative source provides a direction in the aggregation objective that lowers the population variance. Thus FACE-HD preserves target-only validity while allowing precision gains from source sites that are sufficiently transportable to the target population.

\section{Simulation Study}\label{sec:simulations}

We evaluate FACE-HD on the negative-transfer data-generating process of \citet{han2025federated} (Section~5.1), adapted to the high-dimensional, sparse, federated regime targeted here. Each replicate has one target site ($R=t$) with $N_t$ observations and $K$ source sites ($R=s_1,\ldots,s_K$) with sample sizes $N_{s_1},\ldots,N_{s_K}$ (total $N_{\mathrm{all}}$); the main line uses $K=2$ with balanced site sizes. Cross-site heterogeneity enters only through a site-specific covariate law (covariate shift) and the site-specific treatment effect defined below; site membership is fixed by block rather than drawn from a site-assignment model. We report bias, root mean square error (RMSE), and $95\%$ confidence-interval coverage for the target estimand.

\paragraph{Covariates.}
Each of the $p$ coordinates of $\boldsymbol{X}=(X_1,\ldots,X_p)$ is drawn independently from a skewed-normal law $\mathrm{SN}(\kappa,1,\nu)$ with shared location $\kappa=0.125$ and unit scale, using the representation
\[
X_j=\kappa+\delta\,|Z_1|+\sqrt{1-\delta^2}\,Z_2,\qquad
Z_1,Z_2\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1),\qquad
\delta=\nu/\sqrt{1+\nu^2}.
\]
The target site uses $\nu=0$, so its covariates are symmetric, $X_j\sim\mathcal{N}(\kappa,1)$; source site $s_j$ uses skewness $\nu_{s_j}$ equally spaced over $[\nu_{\max}/K,\nu_{\max}]$ with $\nu_{\max}=0.2$, keeping every source bounded away from the target so that the calibration model faces varying covariate shift. The design is sparse: only the first $d=\min(4,p)$ coordinates are active and the remaining $p-d$ are pure noise, which scales the low-dimensional benchmark of \citet{han2025federated} to high dimension.

\paragraph{Propensity and outcome models.}
Let $\psi(u)=\{1+\exp(-u)\}^{-1}$ and let $\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2}$ denote the element-wise square of $\boldsymbol{X}$. Treatment is assigned by $\pi(\boldsymbol{X})=\psi\{\boldsymbol{X}^T\boldsymbol{\alpha}_1+(\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2})^T\boldsymbol{\alpha}_2\}$ and $A\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi(\boldsymbol{X}))$, with the generated propensity clipped to $[0.1,0.9]$ to enforce positivity; over the $d$ active coordinates $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_1$ is equally spaced from $0.5$ to $-0.5$ and $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_2=(-0.5,0,\ldots,0)$, with zeros elsewhere. For a unit in site $r\in\{t,s_1,\ldots,s_K\}$, both potential-outcome arms share the covariate coefficients $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{lin}}=\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{sq}}$, so the treatment effect is carried entirely by a site-specific constant $\Delta_r$ (no within-site effect modification). With $g(\boldsymbol{X})=(\boldsymbol{X}-\kappa\mathbf{1}_p)^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{lin}}+(\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2})^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{sq}}$ denoting the covariate term, the arm-$a$ linear predictor for a unit in site $r$ is
\[
g(\boldsymbol{X})+\Delta_r\,I(a=1),\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The $d$ active coefficient entries of $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{lin}}=\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathrm{sq}}$ are equally spaced from $0.4$ to $1.2$. In the main line (matching the EHR application) outcomes are binary, $Y(a)\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\bigl(\psi\{g(\boldsymbol{X})+\Delta_r\,I(a=1)\}\bigr)$; an optional continuous variant sets $Y(a)=g(\boldsymbol{X})+\Delta_r\,I(a=1)+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ and $\sigma=2\sqrt{5}$. Consistency gives $Y=A\,Y(1)+(1-A)\,Y(0)$.

\paragraph{Negative transfer.}
The target effect is fixed at $\Delta_t=3.0$. The first $n_{\mathrm{dev}}$ source sites are made non-informative by setting $\Delta_{s_j}=\Delta_t+\rho$, while the remaining sources stay informative at $\Delta_{s_j}=\Delta_t$; $n_{\mathrm{dev}}=0$ recovers the all-informative case (deviation level~1 of \citealp{han2025federated}). A deviated source carries an effect different from the target, so its source-assisted estimator $\widehat{\mu}^a_{t,s_j}$ is biased and the adaptive aggregation must shrink its weight---precisely the negative transfer that FACE-HD is designed to control. \Cref{tab:face_dev} lists the deviation gradient we run for $K=2$, an analogue of Table~1 of \citet{han2025federated} over the operating range exercised here.

\paragraph{Misspecification configurations.}
Misspecification corresponds to omitting the quadratic block $\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2}$. In C(1) both nuisance models are correct: the propensity design uses $[\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2}]$ and the outcome design uses $[\boldsymbol{X}-\kappa\mathbf{1}_p,\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2}]$. In C(2) the outcome model is misspecified, using $\boldsymbol{X}$ only while the propensity design keeps $[\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2}]$. In C(3) the propensity model is misspecified, using $\boldsymbol{X}$ only while the outcome design keeps $[\boldsymbol{X}-\kappa\mathbf{1}_p,\boldsymbol{X}^{\circ 2}]$. In C(4) both drop the quadratic block and use $\boldsymbol{X}$ only; this fully-misspecified boundary case is defined here but not exercised by the reported runs.

\paragraph{Estimand and operating range.}
The estimand is the target treated-arm mean $\mu^1=\E_t[Y(1)]$, with the TATE following as $\tau=\mu^1-\mu^0$; the target means $\E_t[Y(a)]$ are evaluated by Monte Carlo over the target covariate law $\boldsymbol{X}\sim\mathcal{N}(\kappa\mathbf{1}_p,\mathbf{I}_p)$. The site effect $\Delta_r$ enters the linear predictor, so for continuous outcomes it is additive and $\tau=\Delta_t=3.0$, whereas for the binary main line $\Delta_t=3.0$ is the conditional log-odds effect and the target risk difference $\tau=\E_t[\psi(g(\boldsymbol{X})+\Delta_t)]-\E_t[\psi(g(\boldsymbol{X}))]$ is a probability-scale contrast in $(0,1)$ rather than $3.0$, likewise obtained by Monte Carlo. The main grid fixes $K=2$ and varies $N_{\mathrm{all}}\in\{3000,6000\}$ (per-site $N_{s_j}\in\{1000,2000\}$), $p\in\{50,100\}$, cross-fitting folds $K_f\in\{5,10\}$, and configurations C(1)--C(3), with binary outcomes over $200$ Monte Carlo replicates. We compare FACE-HD with target-only estimation, sample-size weighting (SS), inverse-variance weighting (IVW), and an exponentially-tilted AIPW baseline (Tilted-AIPW) that aggregates density-ratio-weighted site-specific AIPW estimators by SS. The SS, IVW, and Tilted-AIPW baselines report standard errors from a multiplier (wild) bootstrap, while FACE-HD and the target-only estimator use the closed-form cross-fitted influence-function variance.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

FACE-HD is designed for the setting in which source sites can help estimate a target-site causal effect but cannot share individual-level records. The method combines federated calibration, doubly robust estimation, and adaptive aggregation in a single workflow. The calibration losses are chosen to enforce orthogonality equations, the aggregation objective accounts for covariance induced by shared target data, and the penalty discourages borrowing from sources whose source-assisted estimates disagree with the target-only estimate.

The theory treats a fixed number of source sites and relies on standard high-dimensional nuisance rates, overlap/moment conditions, and a separation condition for exact source selection. These assumptions clarify the intended operating regime: FACE-HD can gain efficiency from transportable sources, remains protected by doubly robust identification for each pairwise estimator, and falls back toward target-only behavior when source discrepancies are large. Extensions to a growing number of sources, weaker local alternatives, and more flexible nuisance learners require additional concentration and empirical-process arguments.

\section{One-Round Communication Algorithms for GLM Outcome Models}

To reduce communication costs, we can devise one-round algorithms. The key idea is to use an initial model estimated on the target site to bootstrap the cross-calibration process at the source sites, removing the need for an initial round of communication from the sources to the target.

This algorithm streamlines the two-round process by using the target-only outcome model, $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^{1}$, as the initial estimate for the cross-calibration procedure. This is a pragmatic trade-off that significantly reduces communication complexity at the cost of a potentially less tailored initialization for each source site.

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Round 1: Single Communication Round}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ computes and sends:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Target-only outcome model parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^{1}$ by minimizing a standard regularized GLM loss on $\mathcal{D}_t$.
            \item The summary statistic $\tildeE_t(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^{1}))$.
            \item The summary statistic $\tildeE_t[\phi(\boldsymbol{X})]$ for the initial site/treatment model in \cref{eq:gamma_init}.
            \item The target site sends all quantities to all source sites $s_j$.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source sites $s_j$ receive info, compute, and send back:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Using the received $\tildeE_t(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\cdot))$, $\tildeE_t[\phi(\boldsymbol{X})]$, and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^{1}$, each source site first computes an initial site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},s_j,1}$ via \cref{eq:gamma_init}, then computes the calibrated $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}$ via \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss}.
            \item Then, it finds its final outcome model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$ by minimizing the calibrated loss in \cref{eq:alpha_calibrated_loss} using the initial site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},s_j,1}$ as plug-in.
            \item It computes the correction term $\widehat{\delta}_{t, s_j}^1$ and source variance $\widehat{V}_{s_j}$.
            \item Each source site $s_j$ sends back to the target: $\widehat{\delta}_{t, s_j}^1$, $N_{s_j}$, $\widehat{V}_{s_j}$, and the final outcome model parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ receives info and performs final aggregation:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item The target computes its target-only estimates $\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}$ and $\widehat{V}_{ot}$.
            \item For each source site $s_j$, using the received $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$ and $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^1$, it assembles the final estimator $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$.

            \item Finally, it solves the optimization problem in \cref{eq:final_opt} to find the optimal aggregation weights $\boldsymbol{\eta}^*$ and compute the final estimate $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}}$.
        \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithmic}

\section{Fold-Level Details for Two-Level Cross-Fitting}\label{app:two_level_cf}

\Cref{alg:facehd-compact} in the main Method section already gives the recommended two-round FACE-HD estimator with two-level cross-fitting. This Supplemental Material section is not a separate robustness add-on; its purpose is to spell out the fold-level bookkeeping compressed in \cref{alg:facehd-compact} and to record the corresponding communication-saving one-round variant.

The two-level construction adapts the cross-fitting strategy of \citet{hou2025efficient}. It uses a nested sample-splitting scheme to ensure that the data used to train an initial nuisance plug-in for an observation is disjoint from the data used for its calibrated loss contribution. As in the SMMAL calibration construction, calibration is carried out over all secondary folds rather than over one held-out fold: for each outer fold $k_1$, every $k_2\neq k_1$ supplies one calibration-fold loss, the corresponding initial fits use the out-of-two-folds data $\mathcal{D}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{k_2})$, and a single calibrated nuisance estimator is obtained by minimizing the average of these fold-specific losses. This separation mitigates overfitting bias in the sequential nuisance updates and supports the Neyman-orthogonal expansion used in the theory.

\subsection{Calibrated Loss Functions}\label{app:calibrated_losses}

The core of the two-level cross-fitting method is the use of specially designed "calibrated" loss functions for refining the nuisance models. These losses are constructed to make the final TATE estimator insensitive to small estimation errors in the nuisance parameters. Following the principles in \citep{smucler2019unifying} and \citep{tan2020model}, we introduce a calibration step after obtaining robust initial estimates.

The truncation function, GLM notation, and calibrated losses are defined earlier in \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss,eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}. The same loss definitions are used foldwise in the two-level cross-fitting construction below.
For a fixed outer fold $k_1$, let $\ell_{\gamma,\mathrm{cal}}^{(k_2)}$ and $\ell_{\alpha,\mathrm{cal}}^{(k_2)}$ denote the fold-specific versions of \cref{eq:gamma_calibrated_loss,eq:alpha_calibrated_loss}, with $\tildeE_t$ and $\tildeE_{s_j}$ replaced by $\tildeE_{t,k_2}$ and $\tildeE_{s_j,k_2}$ and with plug-in initial estimators trained on $\mathcal{D}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{k_2})$. The calibrated nuisance estimators for evaluating fold $k_1$ are
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}^{(-k_1)}
=
\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
\frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2\neq k_1}
\ell_{\gamma,\mathrm{cal}}^{(k_2)}
\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}^1\right),
\]
and
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1)}
=
\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
\frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2\neq k_1}
\ell_{\alpha,\mathrm{cal}}^{(k_2)}
\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}\right).
\]
Thus the secondary folds enter as loss contributions in one calibrated optimization problem, rather than as separate calibrated parameter estimates to be averaged.
In the two-level algorithm, the calibrated $\gamma$-loss uses $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{init}}$ while the calibrated $\alpha$-loss uses $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\text{init}}$. In the Supplemental Material, we assume these initial estimators converge to the same population nuisance targets as the final calibrated estimators, so this plug-in order does not change the first-order asymptotic results.

\subsection{Expanded Two-Round Algorithm with Two-Level Cross-Fitting}\label{app:expanded_two_round}
This is the expanded fold-level version of \cref{alg:facehd-compact}. It makes explicit the secondary calibration folds, the training samples used for the initial plug-ins, and the summaries exchanged in each communication round.

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialization:} At each site $R \in \{t, s_1, \ldots, s_K\}$, randomly partition the local data $\mathcal{D}_R$ into $K_f$ disjoint, equally sized folds: $\{\mathcal{D}_{R,k}\}_{k=1}^{K_f}$. The following steps describe the computation for a single main estimation fold $k_1 \in \{1, \ldots, K_f\}$. The SMMAL-style calibration layer constructs one fold-specific initial plug-in for every secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$.

\STATE \textbf{Round 1: Initial Nuisance Models}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Source sites $s_j$ compute and send:} For each secondary fold $k_2 \in \{1, \ldots, K_f\} \setminus \{k_1\}$, source site $s_j$ computes:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item An initial outcome model parameter $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}^{1}$ by minimizing the standard GLM loss from \cref{eq:alpha_init} using all its local data \textbf{except} folds $k_1$ and $k_2$, i.e., on data $\mathcal{D}_{s_j} \setminus (\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1} \cup \mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2})$.
        \item[\textbf{Note:}] The initial site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\text{init}}$ is \textbf{not} computed here as it requires target site information.
    \end{itemize}
    It sends the set of $K_f-1$ initial outcome models $\{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}^{1}\}_{k_2 \neq k_1}$ to the target site.

	    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ receives and sends back:} For each source $s_j$ and each secondary fold $k_2 \neq k_1$, the target computes two summary statistics, separating the calibration fold from the training folds:
	    \begin{itemize}
	        \item For the \textit{calibrated} site/treatment model, a calibration-fold summary $\tildeE_{t,k_2}[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}^{1})]$.
	        \item For the \textit{initial} site/treatment model, a training-fold summary $\tildeE_{t,-(k_1,k_2)}[\phi(\boldsymbol{X})]$ computed on $\mathcal{D}_t\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{t,k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{t,k_2})$.
	    \end{itemize}
    It sends the corresponding set of $K_f-1$ pairs of summaries back to source site $s_j$.
\end{enumerate}

\STATE \textbf{Round 2: Calibrated Nuisance Models and Estimation}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Source sites $s_j$ receive target info and compute:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For each secondary fold $k_2 \neq k_1$, the source computes the fold-specific initial site/treatment model:
        \begin{itemize}
	            \item \textbf{First}, compute the initial site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}$ by minimizing its standard loss (\cref{eq:gamma_init}) using the received training-fold summary $\tildeE_{t,-(k_1,k_2)}[\phi(\boldsymbol{X})]$ and its own data from $\mathcal{D}_{s_j} \setminus (\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1} \cup \mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2})$.
        \end{itemize}
        \item After all fold-specific initial plug-ins are available, it solves the fold-summed calibrated objectives over the local calibration folds $\{\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2}:k_2\neq k_1\}$:
        \[
        \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,(k_1)}
        =
        \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
        \frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2\neq k_1}
        \ell_{\gamma,\mathrm{cal}}^{(k_2)}
        \left(\boldsymbol{\gamma};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}^1\right),
        \]
        \[
        \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^{1}
        =
        \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
        \frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2\neq k_1}
        \ell_{\alpha,\mathrm{cal}}^{(k_2)}
        \left(\boldsymbol{\alpha};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}\right).
        \]
        \item Using these fold-summed calibrated parameters, it computes the correction term $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^1$ and source variance $\widehat{V}_{s_j,(k_1)}$ on its own main estimation data from fold $\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1}$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source sites $s_j$ send back:} The fold-summed calibrated parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^{1}$, the correction term $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^1$, and variance component $\widehat{V}_{s_j,(k_1)}$.

    \item \textbf{Additional source summaries for aggregation-penalty validation:} If the aggregation penalty $\lambda$ is selected by the inner validation rule rather than fixed in advance, each source also prepares split-specific summaries for every inner validation fold $k_2\neq k_1$. Let
    \[
    \mathcal{T}_{k_1,-k_2}
    \coloneqq
    \mathcal{D}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{k_2})
    \]
    denote the inner-training data. Using nuisance estimates trained only on $\mathcal{T}_{k_1,-k_2}$, source $s_j$ evaluates weighted residual summaries on both the inner-training portion and the held-out inner-validation fold:
    \[
    \widehat{\delta}^{1,\mathrm{tr}}_{t,s_j,(k_1,k_2)},\quad
    \widehat{V}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)},\quad
    N^{\mathrm{tr}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}
    \]
    and
    \[
    \widehat{\delta}^{1,\mathrm{val}}_{t,s_j,(k_1,k_2)},\quad
    \widehat{V}^{\mathrm{val}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)},\quad
    N^{\mathrm{val}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}.
    \]
    It sends these fold-level summaries, together with the corresponding inner-training outcome parameter $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1,-k_2)}$, to the target. The site/treatment parameter $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}^{(-k_1,-k_2)}$ is used locally to form the weighted residual summaries and need not be sent unless one wants the target to reconstruct source-side influence values explicitly. No individual-level validation records are shared.

	    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ computes fold-specific estimate:} Using the received information and its own data, the target computes:
	    \begin{itemize}
			        \item \textit{Inner-fold tuning of the aggregation penalty:} Within $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$, apply the FACE-style validation rule: for each secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$, solve \cref{eq:final_opt} on training summaries computed from $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_2}$, using the source-side training summaries $\{\widehat{\delta}^{1,\mathrm{tr}}_{t,s_j,(k_1,k_2)},\widehat{V}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)},N^{\mathrm{tr}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}\}_{j=1}^K$ and target-side training summaries computed locally. Then plug the resulting weights into the validation penalized criterion computed on $\mathcal{D}_{k_2}$, using the source-side validation summaries $\{\widehat{\delta}^{1,\mathrm{val}}_{t,s_j,(k_1,k_2)},\widehat{V}^{\mathrm{val}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)},N^{\mathrm{val}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}\}_{j=1}^K$ and target-side validation summaries computed locally. Average these validation criteria over $k_2$ to select $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$.
	        \item \textit{Weight optimization:} Recompute the variance--covariance components on the full outer-training set $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$ using out-of-inner-fold nuisance estimates, and solve \cref{eq:final_opt} with $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$ to get $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$.
		        \item \textit{Estimation:} Using the fold-summed calibrated nuisances $\widehat{\vartheta}^{(-k_1)}$ and the weights $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$, compute the fold-specific aggregated estimate $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg},k_1}$ and per-sample IF values on the evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$.
	    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\STATE \textbf{Final Aggregation:} The entire process is repeated for each main fold $k_1=1, \ldots, K_f$. The final point estimate is the average: $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}} = \frac{1}{K_f}\sum_{k_1=1}^{K_f} \widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg},k_1}$.

To ensure that the aggregation weights $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$ are independent of the evaluation data $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$, both the tuning of $\lambda$ and the final weight optimization are carried out inside the training set $\mathcal{T}_{k_1} = \mathcal{D} \setminus \mathcal{D}_{k_1}$. Specifically, set $M = K_f - 1$ inner folds that coincide with the secondary folds $\{k_2 : k_2 \neq k_1\}$. For each inner validation fold $k_2$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Use inner-training summaries from $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k_2}$ to solve the penalized aggregation problem over the candidate grid $\Lambda$, producing $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1,-k_2)}(\lambda)$ for each $\lambda$. On the source side these summaries consist of the fold-level correction terms $\widehat{\delta}^{1,\mathrm{tr}}_{t,s_j,(k_1,k_2)}$, source variance components $\widehat{V}^{\mathrm{tr}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}$, source sample sizes $N^{\mathrm{tr}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}$, and the inner-training outcome parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1,-k_2)}$ needed by the target to compute target-side means and covariances.
    \item Use the same nuisance estimates, trained without $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{k_2}$, to compute validation summaries on $\mathcal{D}_{k_2}$. On the source side this gives $\widehat{\delta}^{1,\mathrm{val}}_{t,s_j,(k_1,k_2)}$, $\widehat{V}^{\mathrm{val}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}$, and $N^{\mathrm{val}}_{s_j,(k_1,k_2)}$; on the target side, the target computes the corresponding target-only IF, target-side regression components, and shared-target covariance terms locally.
    \item Evaluate $Q_{\mathrm{val},k_2}(\lambda)$ by plugging $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1,-k_2)}(\lambda)$ into the validation penalized criterion in \cref{eq:lambda_validation_loss}.
\end{enumerate}
The validation criteria are averaged across the $K_f - 1$ inner folds to select $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$. The final outer-training components are then averaged as
\[
\widehat{V}_{\cdot}^{(-k_1)} \coloneqq \frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2 \neq k_1} \widehat{V}_{\cdot,(k_1,k_2)},
\]
and similarly for $\widehat{C}_{ot}^{(-k_1)}$ and $\widehat{C}_{\mathrm{cross}}^{(-k_1)}$. The weights $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$ are obtained by solving \cref{eq:final_opt} with these components and $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$. Because the entire tuning and weight-estimation procedure uses only $\mathcal{T}_{k_1}$, $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(-k_1)}$ is independent of the evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$.

The point estimate and variance are derived from the \emph{same} set of per-observation uncentered influence functions. Under the standard multi-site sampling regime (independent samples per site with fixed sampling fractions), the variance of the overall mean depends on \emph{within-site} variation only. For each observation $i$ in evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_{k(i)}$, define
\[
\widehat{\Phi}_i \coloneqq
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{N_{\mathrm{all}}}{N_t}\biggl[(1 - \textstyle\sum_j \widehat{\eta}_j^{(-k(i))})\,\widetilde{Y}_{ot,i} + \textstyle\sum_j \widehat{\eta}_j^{(-k(i))}\,\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i);\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k(i))})\biggr], & i \in \text{target},\\[8pt]
\displaystyle\frac{N_{\mathrm{all}}}{N_{s_j}}\,\widehat{\eta}_j^{(-k(i))}\,w(\boldsymbol{X}_i;\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}^{(-k(i))})\bigl(Y_i - \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i);\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k(i))})\bigr), & i \in \text{source } s_j,
\end{cases}
\]
Here $N_{\mathrm{all}} = N_t + \sum_j N_{s_j}$, $\widetilde{Y}_{ot,i}$ is the \emph{uncentered} AIPW pseudo-outcome for the target-only estimator,
\[
\widetilde{Y}_{ot,i}
  \coloneqq \frac{A_i Y_i}{\widehat{\pi}_{ot}(\boldsymbol{X}_i)}
  - \biggl(\frac{A_i}{\widehat{\pi}_{ot}(\boldsymbol{X}_i)} - 1\biggr)
    \psi\bigl(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i);\,\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^{1,(-k(i))}\bigr),
\]
so that the centered IF defined in \cref{eq:if_target_only} satisfies
$\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} = \widetilde{Y}_{ot,i} - \widehat{\mu}^1_{ot,k(i)}$
(the fold-specific target-only point estimate).
Likewise, the centered target-site component \cref{eq:if_target_component} satisfies
$\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i} = \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}_i);\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k(i))}) - \tildeE_{t,k(i)}[\psi(\cdot)]$.
All nuisance parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k)}$, $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}^{(-k)}$, and $\widehat{\pi}_{ot}^{(-k)}$ are trained on data excluding the evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_k$ (with the fold-summed calibration over secondary folds as described above).
Note that the source contribution is written in uncentered form; subtracting the fold-specific correction term $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j}^{(-k)} = \tildeE_{s_j,k}[w(Y-\psi)]$ yields the centered influence component $\widehat{\xi}_{t,s_j,i}$ defined in \cref{eq:if_source_component}.

The point estimate and variance are then derived from the \emph{same} set $\{\widehat{\Phi}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{all}}}$:
\begin{align*}
\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}} &= \bar{\Phi} \coloneqq \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{all}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{all}}} \widehat{\Phi}_i, \\[4pt]
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}\bigl(\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}}\bigr)
&= \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{all}}^2} \sum_{g\in\{t,s_1,\ldots,s_K\}}\;\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_g} \bigl(\widehat{\Phi}_i - \bar{\Phi}_g\bigr)^2,
\qquad \bar{\Phi}_g \coloneqq \frac{1}{N_g}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_g}\widehat{\Phi}_i.
\end{align*}
This within-site centered form is the natural plug-in estimator for the variance of a mean under independent multi-sample (site-stratified) sampling; global centering would add an ANOVA between-site term that is not sampling variability in this regime. With balanced folds, $\bar{\Phi}$ coincides with the fold-average $(1/K_f)\sum_k \widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg},k}$.
\end{algorithmic}

\subsection{One-Round Algorithm with Two-Level Cross-Fitting}\label{app:one_round_cf}
This algorithm adapts the two-level split to the more communication-efficient one-round protocol. The target site bootstraps the entire cross-fitting process by computing and sending all necessary initial models and summary statistics in a single round.

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialization:} All sites partition their local data into $K_f$ folds: $\{\mathcal{D}_{R,k}\}_{k=1}^{K_f}$. The following describes the computation for a single main estimation fold $k_1$.

\STATE \textbf{Round 1: Single Communication Round}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ computes and sends:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For each secondary fold $k_2$ where $k_2 \neq k_1$:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Computes a preliminary target-only outcome model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}^{1}$ on its local data from all folds \textbf{except} $k_1$ and $k_2$.
	            \item Computes the corresponding summary statistic for the \textit{calibrated} model: $$\tildeE_{t,k_2}[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}^{1})]$$ on its local fold $\mathcal{D}_{t,k_2}$.
	            \item Computes the summary statistic for the \textit{initial} model: $\tildeE_{t,-(k_1,k_2)}[\phi(\boldsymbol{X})]$ on $\mathcal{D}_t\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{t,k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{t,k_2})$.
        \end{itemize}
        \item It sends the full set of $(K_f-1)$ initial models and their corresponding $(K_f-1)$ pairs of summary statistics (all associated with main fold $k_1$) to all source sites.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source sites $s_j$ receive info, compute, and send back:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For each secondary fold $k_2 \neq k_1$, the source site computes the fold-specific initial site/treatment model:
        \begin{itemize}
	            \item \textbf{First}, compute the initial site/treatment model $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\text{init},(k_1,k_2)}$ on its local data from $\mathcal{D}_{s_j} \setminus (\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1} \cup \mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2})$ using the received training-fold summary $\tildeE_{t,-(k_1,k_2)}[\phi(\boldsymbol{X})]$.
        \end{itemize}
        \item It then solves the SMMAL-style fold-summed calibrated objectives over all secondary folds $k_2\neq k_1$ to get the final nuisance models for the main fold $k_1$: $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^{1}$ and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,(k_1)}$.
        \item It computes the correction term $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^1$ and variance $\widehat{V}_{s_j,(k_1)}$ on its main estimation data from fold $\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1}$.
        \item Each source sends back the fold-summed calibrated parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^{1}$ along with $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,(k_1)}^1$ and $\widehat{V}_{s_j,(k_1)}$.
    \end{itemize}
	    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ receives info and computes fold-specific estimate:}
	    \begin{itemize}
			        \item Using the received fold-summed calibrated parameters and inner-fold target-only models, the target applies the FACE-style inner validation rule to select $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$ without using $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$.
	        \item It solves the final weight optimization on the outer-training summaries with $\widehat{\lambda}^{(-k_1)}$ and computes the fold-specific aggregated estimate $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg},k_1}$ and per-sample IF values on the evaluation fold $\mathcal{D}_{k_1}$.
	    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\STATE \textbf{Final Aggregation:} As before, the process is repeated for each main fold $k_1=1, \ldots, K_f$. The point estimate is $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}} = \frac{1}{K_f}\sum_{k_1} \widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg},k_1}$, and the variance is computed using the sample-level DML formula with inner $M$-fold weights, as described in the two-round algorithm above.
\end{algorithmic}

\subsection{Linear Outcome One-Round Algorithm with Two-Level Cross-Fitting}\label{app:linear_one_round_cf}

When the outcome model is linear, $\psi\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X});\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}^T\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, the one-round algorithm above simplifies because
\[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\psi\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X});\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}=\phi(\boldsymbol{X})
\]
does not depend on an initial outcome parameter. Thus the target does not need to send fold-specific initial outcome models to the sources. It only sends fold-specific covariate means needed for calibration, while the source sites compute the linear calibration and weighted least-squares updates locally. The outer fold $k_1$ and secondary fold $k_2$ are still kept separate, so this is the cross-fitted linear specialization of the one-round estimator, not the non-cross-fitted sufficient-statistics shortcut.

For a fixed target-source pair and the treated arm, define the secondary-fold linear calibration loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma_loss_linear_cf}
\ell_{\gamma,\mathrm{lin}}^{(k_1,k_2)}(\boldsymbol{\gamma})
=
\tildeE_{t,k_2}\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}^T\boldsymbol{\gamma}
+
\tildeE_{s_j,k_2}\!\left[I(A=1)\exp\{-\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T\boldsymbol{\gamma}\}\right]
+
\lambda_{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}\|_1 .
\end{equation}
This is exactly the GLM calibrated $\gamma$-loss with $\psi'(\cdot)=1$. For the outcome update, each secondary fold uses an initial tilting fit $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}$ trained on $\mathcal{D}_{s_j}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2})$ with the corresponding target training-fold mean $\tildeE_{t,-(k_1,k_2)}\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}$. The fold-specific weighted least-squares loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha_loss_linear_cf}
\ell_{\alpha,\mathrm{lin}}^{(k_1,k_2)}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
=
\tildeE_{s_j,k_2}\!\left[
I(A=1)\exp\{-\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}\}
\{Y-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^T\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}^2
\right]
+
\lambda_{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|_1 .
\end{equation}
The nuisance estimators used on the held-out outer fold are obtained by minimizing the fold-summed versions of these two losses:
\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}^{(-k_1)}
\in
\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
\frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2\neq k_1}
\ell_{\gamma,\mathrm{lin}}^{(k_1,k_2)}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}),
\qquad
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1)}
\in
\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
\frac{1}{K_f-1}\sum_{k_2\neq k_1}
\ell_{\alpha,\mathrm{lin}}^{(k_1,k_2)}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}).
\]

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Initialization:} All sites use the same outer and secondary folds as in \cref{app:one_round_cf}. The steps below describe one outer evaluation fold $k_1$ and the treated arm; the control arm is obtained by replacing $I(A=1)$ with $I(A=0)$ and using the corresponding nuisance parameters.
\STATE \textbf{Round 1: Single Communication Round}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ computes and sends:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For each secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$, the calibration-fold mean $\tildeE_{t,k_2}\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}$ entering \cref{eq:gamma_loss_linear_cf}.
        \item For each secondary fold $k_2\neq k_1$, the training-fold mean $\tildeE_{t,-(k_1,k_2)}\{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}$ used to compute the initial tilting fit for \cref{eq:alpha_loss_linear_cf}.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source site $s_j$ receives summaries, computes, and sends back:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item For each $k_2\neq k_1$, compute $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{init},(k_1,k_2)}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{s_j}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_2})$ using the received training-fold target mean.
        \item Solve the fold-summed linear calibration and weighted least-squares problems in \cref{eq:gamma_loss_linear_cf,eq:alpha_loss_linear_cf} to obtain $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}^{(-k_1)}$ and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1)}$.
        \item On the held-out source fold $\mathcal{D}_{s_j,k_1}$, compute the source correction $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,k_1}^1$ and source-side variance using the cross-fitted nuisance pair.
        \item Send $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1)}$, $\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,k_1}^1$, the source-side variance, and $N_{s_j,k_1}$ to the target.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ receives info and computes fold-specific aggregation:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Form $\widehat{\mu}_{t,s_j,k_1}^1=\tildeE_{t,k_1}\{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1)T}\phi(\boldsymbol{X})\}+\widehat{\delta}_{t,s_j,k_1}^1$.
        \item Compute target-side variances and cross-source covariances locally on $\mathcal{D}_{t,k_1}$. If $\widehat{\Sigma}_{t,k_1}$ is the covariance matrix of $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$ on the target evaluation fold, then the target-side source covariance has the linear form $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,(-k_1)T}\widehat{\Sigma}_{t,k_1}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_\ell}^{1,(-k_1)}$.
        \item Select aggregation weights inside the outer-training sample as in the FACE-style validation rule, apply them to the held-out fold, and repeat across $k_1=1,\ldots,K_f$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithmic}

\section{One-Round Communication Algorithm for Linear Outcome Models}
When the outcome models are linear, i.e., $\psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \boldsymbol{\alpha}^T \phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, the framework simplifies significantly. Specifically, the gradient of the outcome model, $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} \psi(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}); \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, does not depend on the parameter $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$. This breaks the iterative dependency between the outcome and site/treatment models, allowing for a highly efficient one-round estimation at the source sites. The strategy is for the target site to send sufficient statistics that allow source sites to locally perform a sequential estimation.

\subsection{Derivations for One-Round Linear Algorithm}

\subsubsection{Variance and Covariance}

Here we first derive the key formulas that allow variance and covariance terms involving target site data to be computed efficiently using pre-calculated sufficient statistics in the linear model case. For notational simplicity, we write $\boldsymbol{X}$ in place of $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$ throughout this section.

The target-side variance for the estimator from source $s_j$ is the variance of its influence function component on the target data. By definition, and noting that the mean of the influence function component is zero:
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{V}_{t,s_j} = \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i})^2
\end{equation*}
For a linear model, $\psi(\boldsymbol{X}_i; \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \boldsymbol{\alpha}^T \boldsymbol{X}_i$. The influence function component is:
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i} = \psi(\boldsymbol{X}_i; \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1) - \tildeE_t[\psi(\boldsymbol{X}; \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1)] = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}\boldsymbol{X}_i - \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}\bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)
\end{equation*}
Squaring this term gives a scalar, which can be expressed as a quadratic form:
\begin{equation*}
(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i})^2 = \left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)\right) \left((\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}\right) = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}
\end{equation*}
Summing over all $i \in \mathcal{D}_t$ and dividing by $N_t$, the central term becomes the sample covariance matrix of the covariates at the target site, $\widehat{\Sigma}_t$:
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{V}_{t,s_j} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \left( \frac{1}{N_t} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t} (\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)^T \right) \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \widehat{\Sigma}_t \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}
\end{equation*}

This term is the covariance between the target-only influence function and the target-side component from source $s_j$.
\begin{align*}
\widehat{C}_{ot,s_j} &= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} - \bar{\widehat{\varphi}}_{ot})(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i}) \\
&= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} - \bar{\widehat{\varphi}}_{ot})\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)\right)
\end{align*}
Since $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}$ is constant with respect to the sum, we can factor it out:
\begin{align*}
\widehat{C}_{ot,s_j} &= \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \left( \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t}(\widehat{\varphi}_{ot,i} - \bar{\widehat{\varphi}}_{ot})(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t) \right) \\
&= \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \widehat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{ot,X}
\end{align*}
where $\widehat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{ot,X}$ is the pre-computed vector of covariances between the target-only IF and the covariates.

This term is needed by the target site for the final aggregation. It requires the parameter vectors from two different source sites, $s_j$ and $s_k$.
\begin{align*}
\widehat{C}_{t,s_j,s_k} &= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t} (\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_j,i})(\widehat{\zeta}_{t,s_k,i}) \\
&= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t} \left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)\right) \left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_k}^{1,T}(\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)\right)
\end{align*}
Rewriting the second scalar term as its transpose allows us to form the quadratic expression:
\begin{align*}
\widehat{C}_{t,s_j,s_k} &= \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t} \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} (\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t) (\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_k}^{1} \\
&= \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \left( \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_t} (\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t) (\boldsymbol{X}_i - \bar{\boldsymbol{X}}_t)^T \right) \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_k}^{1} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \widehat{\Sigma}_t \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_k}^{1}
\end{align*}
This shows that once the target has $\widehat{\Sigma}_t$ and receives the parameter vectors $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$ and $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_k}^{1}$, it can compute this final covariance term.

\subsubsection{Loss Functions}
For the linear case, the general loss functions from the main text specialize to the following forms. This allows for a sequential estimation procedure: first $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ is estimated to balance the raw covariates $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, and then $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ is estimated using weights derived from $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$.

The loss for $\boldsymbol{\gamma}$ becomes a direct covariate balancing objective using an exponential tilting formulation. As it only depends on the features $\phi(\boldsymbol{X})$, it can be minimized first without knowledge of $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$.
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gamma_loss_linear_corrected}
\ell(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}) = \left( \tildeE_t \left[ \phi(\boldsymbol{X}) \right] \right)^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1} + \tildeE_{s_j} \left[ I(A=1) e^{-\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}} \right] + \lambda_{\gamma} \|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{s_j,1}\|_1
\end{equation}
The term $\tildeE_t(\phi(\boldsymbol{X}))$ is the mean covariate vector $\bar{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_t$ sent from the target site.

The outcome model parameters are estimated using weighted least squares (Lasso Regression), which is the specific form of the general GLM loss for linear models. The weights are derived from the calibration/tilting model estimated in the previous step.
\begin{equation} \label{eq:alpha_loss_linear_corrected}
\ell(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1) = \tildeE_{s_j}\left[ \frac{I(A=1)}{e^{\phi(\boldsymbol{X})^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}}} (Y - \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^{1,T}\phi(\boldsymbol{X}))^2 \right] + \lambda_{\alpha} \|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{t,s_j}^1\|_1
\end{equation}

\subsection{Algorithm Steps}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Round 1: Single Communication Round}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ computes and sends:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Target-only estimates: $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ot}^1$, $\widehat{\mu}^1_{ot}$, and $\widehat{V}_{ot}$.
            \item Sufficient statistics from $\mathcal{D}_t$: the covariate mean $\bar{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_t$, the covariate covariance matrix $\widehat{\Sigma}_t$, the sample size $N_t$, and the covariance vector $\widehat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{ot,X}$.
            \item The target site broadcasts this package of information to all source sites.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source sites $s_j$ receive info, compute, and send back:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Each source site first computes its density ratio parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}$ by minimizing \cref{eq:gamma_loss_linear_corrected} using the received $\bar{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_t$.
            \item Then, it computes its outcome model parameters $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$ by minimizing the weighted least squares loss in \cref{eq:alpha_loss_linear_corrected}, using weights derived from the newly computed $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s_j,1}$.
            \item Using the received statistics, it computes the final estimator $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$, the target-side variance $\widehat{V}_{t,s_j} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \widehat{\Sigma}_t \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1$, and the covariance $\widehat{C}_{ot,s_j} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{ot,X}^T \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^1$.
            \item Using its own data, it computes the source-side variance $\widehat{V}_{s_j}$.
            \item Each source site $s_j$ sends back to the target: $\widehat{\mu}^1_{t,s_j}$, $\widehat{V}_{t,s_j}$, $\widehat{V}_{s_j}$, $\widehat{C}_{ot,s_j}$, $N_{s_j}$, and the parameter vector $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1}$ (needed for cross-site covariances).
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Target site $t$ receives info and performs final aggregation:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item For each pair of source sites $(s_j, s_k)$, the target computes the cross-site covariance: $\widehat{C}_{t,s_j,s_k} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_j}^{1,T} \widehat{\Sigma}_t \widehat{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{t,s_k}^{1}$.
            \item With all estimators and variance-covariance components now available, the target solves the optimization problem in \cref{eq:final_opt} to find the optimal aggregation weights $\boldsymbol{\eta}^*$ and compute the final estimate $\widehat{\mu}^1_{\text{agg}}$.
        \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithmic}
