\section{Refinement: conditional outcome errors and reuse of source designs}
\label{sec:conditional-refinement}

This refinement keeps the residual compatibility construction and changes its
nuisance certificate and sample use. It currently applies to saturated binary
outcome fits whose weighting functions are fitted from $(X,A)$ alone. It does
not automatically apply to outcome-dependent calibrated weights in the full
RoCE fitting procedure. The earlier corner construction remains a comparison.

\subsection{Which valid-subset event is actually needed?}

For each distribution $P$, select, only for the proof, a deterministic truly
valid subset $V_a(P)$ of size $g_a$. Validity is a population property fixed
before sampling; this subset is not estimated or supplied to the algorithm.
Coverage needs a drift guarantee for this unknown subset. It does not require
that the outcome-error event hold simultaneously for every data-selected subset.
A bound uniform over $P$ is sufficient even though its proof event is indexed
by $V_a(P)$. This distinction avoids a combinatorial selection penalty while
retaining the possibility of arbitrarily weak invalid-source shifts. It does
not justify choosing the proof subset after observing fitted errors.

Let $N_{xa}$ and $S_{xa}$ be target training counts and outcome successes,
with $\hat m_a(x)=(S_{xa}+1/2)/(N_{xa}+1)$. Conditional on all relevant
covariate and treatment designs, the successes in different cells are
independent binomials with means $N_{xa}m_t^a(x)$. For a design-fixed vector
$c$, its drift is
\[
 B(c)=\sum_xc_x\{m_t^a(x)-\hat m_a(x)\}.
\]
Suppose a design-measurable certificate gives $|c_x|\le C_x$. Define
\begin{equation}
 b(C,N;\delta)=\frac12\sum_x\frac{C_x}{N_{xa}+1}
 +\sqrt{\frac12\log(2/\delta)
                \sum_x\frac{N_{xa}C_x^2}{(N_{xa}+1)^2}}.
 \label{eq:v4-scalar-radius}
\end{equation}
On the design certificate, $|B(c)|\le b(C,N;\delta)$ except with conditional
probability at most $\delta$. The first term protects the smoothing bias;
the second follows by Hoeffding's lemma for the weighted outcome errors.
There is no union over outcome-box corners. Crucially, the true vector $c$
must be fixed given designs: using weights trained on these same outcomes
would invalidate this conditional argument.

For sources the vector is
\[
 c_{V_a,x}=g_a^{-1}\sum_{j\in V_a}q_j^a(x)p_j(x,A=a)-p_t(x).
\]
For the target it is $c_{t,a,x}=q_t^a(x)p_t(x,A=a)-p_t(x)$.
Bound these design coefficients first, then apply
\eqref{eq:v4-scalar-radius} to the two source and two target directions.
Their shared training outcomes need not be independent across directions;
allocate the outcome error across these four scalar events.

\subsection{A certificate requiring no supplied source probability lower bound}

For each cell and arm, the source design scores are
$Z_{ji}^{xa}=q_j^a(x)\ind(X_{ji}=x,A_{ji}=a)$.
Use the pooled empirical mean and direct dispersion bounds from the main
construction. If $[L_{xa},U_{xa}]$ contains the all-source mean of their
expectations and $Q_{xa}$ bounds their dispersion, every $g_a$-subset mean
lies in
\[
 [L_{xa}-\sqrt{(K-g_a)Q_{xa}/g_a},\;
  U_{xa}+\sqrt{(K-g_a)Q_{xa}/g_a}].
\]
Subtract the simultaneous target cell-probability interval to obtain a bound
$C_{V_a,x}$ on the coefficient. Target coefficients are bounded using the
same joint $(X,A)$ and marginal $X$ probability region. Optimizing each
cell's two probabilities is a two-variable linear problem.

Allocate $\delta_B/5$ to target design probabilities,
$2\delta_B/5$ to the $2L$ source design mean/dispersion pairs, and
$2\delta_B/5$ to the four scalar outcome events. With $\delta_B=.005$ these
are $.001,.002,.002$. The coefficient event protects all subsets, but the
conditional outcome event only protects the fixed true valid subset required
by the proof. Complexity is linear in the number of cells rather than
exponential in their number. Statistical bounds can nevertheless deteriorate
with sparse cell counts; computational scaling is not high-dimensional theory.

\subsection{An optional source-training MGF certificate}

An alternative exploits the exact half-count weight formula
\[
 q_j^a(x)=\hat p_t(x)\frac{n_s+L}{N_{jxa}+1/2},\qquad
 N_{jxa}\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n_s,p_j(x,A=a)).
\]
This option requires a \emph{declared} lower bound $p_j(x,A=a)\ge p_*$ for
valid sources. It is additional quantitative overlap information, not a bound
estimated from oracle truths. Let $Q_p=(n_s+L)p/(B_p+1/2)$.
For $p\in[l,u]$, binomial stochastic ordering gives the MGF envelopes
\begin{align*}
 \E e^{t(Q_p-1)}
 &\le\E_{B_l}e^{t\{(n_s+L)u/(B_l+1/2)-1\}},\\
 \E e^{-t(Q_p-1)}
 &\le\E_{B_u}e^{-t\{(n_s+L)l/(B_u+1/2)-1\}}.
\end{align*}
Take the maximum over intervals covering $[p_*,1]$ to obtain
$M_+(t),M_-(t)$. These are outer bounds for every probability between grid
points, not evaluations that assume the maximizer lies on a grid.
Independent training across the fixed $g_a$ valid sources and Chernoff's
inequality give
\[
 1-h_-\le g_a^{-1}\sum_{j\in V_a}Q_{p_j}\le1+h_+,
 \quad
 h_\pm=\min_{t\in\mathcal T}
 \frac{\log M_\pm(t)+g_a^{-1}\log(2/\delta)}{t}.
\]
The positive tilt set is deterministic. This minimization therefore does not
need an extra data-selection error allowance. Multiplying by $\hat p_t(x)$
and subtracting the target probability interval bounds the source coefficient
for \eqref{eq:v4-scalar-radius}. A source outcome-box/corner version is also
retained to isolate the gain from conditional outcome concentration.

The numerical experiment uses 2,048 logarithmically spaced probability bins
and 100 deterministic tilts, with $p_*\in\{.02,.04,.06\}$ as prespecified
sensitivity settings. Stochastic bracketing supplies the mathematical guarantee;
floating-point evaluation uses log-sum-exp and an outward cushion and is not
an interval-arithmetic proof. Unknown half-count fitting bias can persist when
$n_s$ is fixed even as $K$ grows. Neither this bound nor a fixed numerical grid
establishes a sharp joint $(n,K)$ efficiency rate.

\subsection{The direct-dispersion procedure can reuse design observations}

In the previous diagnostic, each source used 500 training, 250 pilot and 250
evaluation patients. Direct dispersion uses fixed training-determined score
ranges and does not select frequencies or variance calibrators from a pilot.
Its score-mean and dispersion events hold conditional on training.
The nuisance-certificate event can use the same held-out $(X,A)$ observations:
a union bound combines these events and does not require their independence.
Thus the direct-dispersion refinement uses 500 training and all 500 held-out
patients for evaluation, and may use the same 500 designs for its nuisance
certificate. The total remains 1,000; a patient used in two summaries is not
counted as a second patient. Fitted weights and predictions are unchanged.

This reuse is not enabled for the current Fourier implementation, whose
frequencies and variance correction condition on an independent pilot.
An explicit API guard prevents transferring the reuse option to that module
without a new argument. The pooled mean and unbiased sample variance are
reconstructed exactly from the two original held-out blocks.

\subsection{A stronger target-only check specific to the finite-stratum model}

Improving source inference must be accompanied by improving its target
reference. First apply \eqref{eq:v4-scalar-radius} to both folds of the
full-target AIPW reference, with $.0025$ nuisance and $.0225$ noise allowance
per fold. In addition, report the saturated full-target plug-in estimator,
which does not need a training/evaluation split in this discrete model.

Let all target observations give counts $N_{xa}$, successes $S_{xa}$ and
empirical cell masses $\hat p_x$. Its estimate is
\[
 \hat\tau_{\rm strat}=\sum_x\hat p_x
 \left\{\frac{S_{x1}+1/2}{N_{x1}+1}
             -\frac{S_{x0}+1/2}{N_{x0}+1}\right\}.
\]
Apply the scalar outcome bound to the stacked coefficient magnitudes
$C_{xa}=\hat p_x$, with failure $\alpha/2$. The remaining composition
error is an average of fixed true cell contrasts in $[-1,1]$, so a second
Hoeffding radius $\sqrt{2\log(4/\alpha)/n_t}$ has failure at most
$\alpha/2$. Add these radii and clip to $[-1,1]$. This is a finite-sample
binary-outcome comparator, not an ordinary normal interval.

The saturated comparator need not be implementable with many continuous
confounders. Nevertheless it is available in the diagnostic DGP and must
be shown there. A gain over the two-fold AIPW certificate alone does not
establish an advantage over reasonable target-only inference in that model.
